02 / The big picture

Manipulation doesn't
have to end at the gripper.

Physical contact ties manipulation to end-effector reach. Directed airflow adds another way to act on an object. BeyondContact connects the hardware, the physics, and robot learning in one framework.

01

A blower beside the gripper

A compact electric ducted fan mounts below the gripper, preserving contact manipulation while adding directed airflow.

02

Physics for robot learning

03

A / THE HARDWARE

Compact hardware.
Two ways to manipulate.

A 40 mm electric ducted fan sits in a 3D-printed enclosure beneath the Piper-L gripper. A magnetic outlet connection supports interchangeable nozzles; the evaluated nozzle has a 31.7 mm outlet.

An ESP32 microcontroller regulates blower output through an electronic speed controller. The gripper remains available for pushing, while the nozzle adds contact-free actuation.

B / THE AIRFLOW MODEL

From a velocity field
to force and torque.

The extension combines experimentally identified centerline decay with Gaussian radial spreading. Incoming airflow acts on sampled surface primitives; their forces sum into a net force and a torque about the object's center of mass.

01Sample the surface

Positions, normals, and areas from primitives or mesh geometry.

02Evaluate the jet

Axial decay and radial spreading at every surface sample.

03Integrate the loads

Sum local forces and moment arms into net force and torque.

04Step the simulation

Apply loads to rigid bodies in Isaac Sim / PhysX.

Model identification and assumptions

The paper compares exponential and shifted-inverse centerline-decay models against CFD and measurements. With the 0.8-FSA nozzle, the shifted-inverse fit achieves a centerline RMSE of 0.895 m/s. For the bare fan, the exponential fit performs better, with an RMSE of 0.606 m/s.

The time-averaged, quasi-steady model assumes an incompressible, axisymmetric free jet. Rotor swirl, instantaneous turbulence, and interactions with nearby environmental surfaces are not explicitly resolved. The extension also supports an approximate reaction force on the robot link carrying the blower.

C / THE LEARNING FRAMEWORK

One action space.
Two learned behaviors.

PPO learns joint-position commands and airflow intensity together. An asymmetric actor–critic uses deployment-available observations for the actor and additional simulation information for the critic.

  • Temporal context: ten-step joint position and velocity histories, object pose, task goal, and previous actions.
  • Robustness: randomize object mass, friction, robot gains, and airflow parameters during training.
  • Task-specific incentives: reward transport and settling for HPB; add heading alignment and contact avoidance for RR.
  • Parallel learning: 4,096 environments and 5,000 training iterations in the reported experiments.

The extension is reusable; this release registers the two Piper-L tasks and their evaluation variants.

Where the current system is limited

The fixed 6-DoF arm limits the nozzle positions and directions available for recovery. HPB failures involved overly aggressive pushing or unintended contact during blowing. The paper identifies a mobile base or a 7-DoF arm as future directions for expanding reach and improving recovery.

The RR task retains the CuboidOrientation identifier in code; its evaluated behavior is airflow-only reorientation. Reported paper settings and release defaults may differ; inspect the checked-in configurations for your run.

Research summary

BeyondContact addresses airflow-assisted robot learning through a gripper-compatible blower and a configurable airflow-interaction extension for Isaac Lab. Experimentally identified jet models produce object forces and torques using surface integration, enabling massively parallel PPO training. Two complementary policies demonstrate hybrid contact–airflow transport and contact-free heading control. Zero-shot real-world experiments report 80% HPB success over 20 trials and a 2.60° mean absolute RR heading error across four target headings.
